\chapter{Requirements Analysis and System Design}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter translates the business framing and technical choices established in the previous chapters into a structured design baseline for \projectname. Its purpose is to identify what the platform must do, which constraints it must respect, who will interact with it, and how the final multi-layer architecture answers those needs. Unlike Chapter 3, which focused on tool selection, this chapter focuses on requirement formalization and on the architectural organization of the implemented solution.

\section{Requirement Elicitation Context}
The requirements of \projectname{} were derived from three complementary sources:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the business objective of creating a distributed customer-data foundation under the broader \initiative{} vision;
    \item the engineering need to ingest, store, process, validate, orchestrate, expose, and monitor heterogeneous customer-related datasets;
    \item the operational reality of a lightweight academic multi-node environment with security hardening requirements.
\end{itemize}

As a result, the chapter does not present abstract requirements in isolation. Each requirement is directly connected to an implemented capability of the platform and to the final distributed deployment model.

\section{Global Logical Architecture}
At the logical level, \projectname{} is organized as a layered customer-data platform rather than as a monolithic data pipeline. Each layer has a precise responsibility in the end-to-end flow:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ingestion layer:} receives and transports the raw input datasets through Kafka.
    \item \textbf{Bronze persistence layer:} stores replayable raw artifacts in HDFS.
    \item \textbf{Raw lakehouse layer:} structures the raw persisted inputs through Spark and Iceberg.
    \item \textbf{Metadata layer:} centralizes table definitions through Hive Metastore.
    \item \textbf{Analytical access layer:} exposes curated tables through Trino.
    \item \textbf{Transformation layer:} applies modeled transformations through dbt-spark.
    \item \textbf{Validation layer:} checks trust and structural integrity through Great Expectations.
    \item \textbf{Orchestration and observability layer:} coordinates and supervises the full process through Airflow, Prometheus, and Grafana.
\end{itemize}

\section{End-to-End Data Flow}
The end-to-end operational flow of the platform can be summarized in the following sequence:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item source datasets are prepared and published through the ingestion workflow;
    \item Kafka receives and organizes the movement through controlled topics;
    \item bronze artifacts are landed in HDFS as replayable raw persistence;
    \item Spark reads the bronze zone and loads structured raw tables into the lakehouse;
    \item Hive Metastore registers the table definitions;
    \item Trino exposes analytical access to the governed tables;
    \item dbt-spark applies business-oriented transformations;
    \item Great Expectations validates the resulting datasets;
    \item Airflow orchestrates and supervises the sequence;
    \item Prometheus and Grafana expose the monitoring evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Design Rationale and Trade-offs}
The final design is the result of deliberate engineering trade-offs rather than maximum-complexity choices:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Distributed proof over oversized scale:} the architecture demonstrates real service distribution while remaining feasible on lightweight academic VMs.
    \item \textbf{Layering over monolithic simplification:} the system is decomposed into clear responsibilities to strengthen maintainability and defense clarity.
    \item \textbf{Security hardening without architectural replacement:} the security layer reinforces the platform without changing its lakehouse logic.
    \item \textbf{Operational clarity over hidden automation:} explicit scripts, bundles, checks, and service roles make the deployment easier to explain and debug.
    \item \textbf{Future readiness over premature AI claims:} the design prepares downstream Customer 360 and AI use cases without pretending that those intelligent modules are already fully implemented.
\end{itemize}

These trade-offs are important for the jury defense because they show that the architecture was shaped by real constraints, not by arbitrary tool accumulation.
