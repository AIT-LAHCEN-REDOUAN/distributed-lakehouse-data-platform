{\Huge\bfseries Abstract\par}
\vspace{0.65cm}

Modern organizations generate large volumes of customer-related data across heterogeneous and fragmented operational environments. Campaign records, behavioral traces, commercial transactions, and profile attributes are produced in different formats, at different rates, and under inconsistent semantic assumptions. When these sources remain isolated, customer information becomes difficult to validate, reconcile, replay, and transform into dependable analytical assets. This fragmentation slows decision-making, prevents a unified customer view, and deprives future intelligent services of a sufficiently reliable data foundation.

This final-year project addresses that problem through the design and implementation of \projectname{}, a distributed customer-data lakehouse platform developed within the broader \initiative{} initiative at \companyname. The completed platform combines replayable Kafka ingestion, HDFS bronze storage, Spark distributed processing, Iceberg and Parquet tables governed through Hive Metastore, and analytical access through Trino. dbt-spark structures the transformation layers, Great Expectations records validation evidence, Airflow orchestrates the workflows, Prometheus and Grafana provide observability, and layered security controls protect service identity, access, communication, and persisted runtime data.

Deployed across a local control plane and three Linux virtual machines, the platform was validated through end-to-end execution scenarios covering ingestion, distributed persistence, transformation, cross-engine analytical access, data-quality checks, orchestration, monitoring, and secure service interaction. The resulting architecture strengthens reproducibility, scalability, traceability, operational reliability, and extensibility. It transforms heterogeneous raw datasets into governed and reusable data products while establishing the engineering foundation for trusted analytics, future Customer 360 capabilities, and subsequent machine-learning developments.
