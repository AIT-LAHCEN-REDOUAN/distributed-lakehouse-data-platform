{\Huge\bfseries Abstract\par}
\vspace{0.65cm}

Modern organizations produce large volumes of customer-related data through heterogeneous and fragmented operational environments. Campaign records, behavioral traces, commercial transactions, and profile attributes are often generated in different formats, at different rhythms, and under different semantic assumptions. When these sources remain isolated, the organization faces a structural difficulty: customer information becomes hard to trust, hard to reconcile, hard to replay, and hard to transform into stable analytical assets. Decision-making is therefore slowed down, cross-source understanding remains partial, and future intelligent services lack a sufficiently reliable data foundation.

This final year project addresses that problematic through the design and implementation of \projectname, a distributed customer data lakehouse platform developed within the broader \initiative{} initiative at \companyname. The objective is not to deliver a final artificial intelligence product, but to establish the engineering foundation required to make future analytics and AI-oriented use cases technically credible. The work therefore focuses on structuring the complete lifecycle of customer-related data, from multi-source ingestion and durable raw persistence to distributed processing, curated analytical organization, quality control, orchestration, observability, and controlled access.

The contribution of the project lies in building a coherent end-to-end platform able to convert heterogeneous raw datasets into governed and reusable data products within a distributed environment. The implemented architecture is designed to improve reproducibility, scalability, traceability, and operational reliability while preserving the flexibility required by evolving customer-data use cases. Beyond the technical implementation, the project demonstrates how a modern data engineering platform can serve as the essential foundation for trusted analytics, future Customer 360 capabilities, and subsequent machine learning developments.
