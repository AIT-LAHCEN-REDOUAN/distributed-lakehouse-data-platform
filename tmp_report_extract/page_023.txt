Different layers of a data platform may therefore adopt different consistency and availability

II.3.2 ACID vs BASE Models
ACID describes transactional guarantees: atomicity, consistency, isolation, and durability.
These properties are important when a group of operations must be committed or rejected
as one unit and concurrent updates must preserve defined invariants. However, not every
stage of a distributed data platform requires the same transactional scope or isolation
level.
BASE, commonly summarized as Basically Available, Soft state, and Eventual consistency,
is a design philosophy that accepts temporary divergence while replicas converge. ACID and
BASE are not symmetric or mutually exclusive alternatives: one characterizes transaction
guarantees, while the other describes a broader availability-oriented operating model.
A modern data platform may combine eventual convergence in one service with ACID
transactions in another, according to the responsibilities of each layer [22].
II.3.3 Data Partitioning and Replication
Partitioning divides data into smaller units so that storage and computation can be
distributed across multiple nodes. It is one of the foundational mechanisms behind modern
large-scale platforms. Without partitioning, a single node becomes a natural bottleneck
for read throughput, write throughput, fault recovery, and transformation execution.
Replication complements partitioning by improving resilience and availability. When
several copies of data or logs exist, the system can continue operating even if one node is
unavailable. Yet replication also increases coordination and storage cost, which is why it
must be treated as an architectural decision rather than a simple implementation detail.
The storage cost of replication can be summarized as:
Seffective≈r×Sraw
where Sraw is the original data volume andr is the replication factor. This relation shows
why replication improves resilience while also increasing storage requirements.
