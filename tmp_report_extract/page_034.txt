Chapter III
Technology Watch
III.1 Introduction
Chapter II established the conceptual foundations of distributed data platforms. This
chapter moves from those foundations to the technological choices implemented in AdOpti-
mizer CDP. Its purpose is not to reproduce product documentation or to present popular
tools in isolation. It explains how the retained stack was evaluated against the project’s
actual constraints, which alternatives were considered, and why the selected technologies
form a coherent platform.
The assessment is retrospective and implementation-based. The resulting platform is
already deployed as a local control plane connected to a distributed data plane composed
of three Linux virtual machines. Consequently, the selected technologies are evaluated
according to their demonstrated contribution to ingestion, distributed storage, processing,
lakehouse governance, analytical access, transformation, quality, orchestration, observabil-
ity, deployment reproducibility, and security.
III.2 Project Constraints and Selection Criteria
The technology choices were guided by the implemented environment rather than by an
abstract enterprise-scale target. The main constraints were the following:
• Distributed execution:the core storage, processing, ingestion, and query services
had to demonstrate real cooperation across VM1, VM2, and VM3.
• Limited infrastructure:the deployment had to remain operable on three resource-
constrained virtual machines without dedicated accelerators.
