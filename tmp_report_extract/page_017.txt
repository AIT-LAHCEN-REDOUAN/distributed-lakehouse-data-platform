AdOptimizer CDP Chapter I. General Project Context
I.3.5 Implemented Scope and Boundaries
The implemented scope covers the active Client 1 bundle composed of theBank Marketing,
Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention, andOnline Retail IIdatasets. Together, these
sources represent campaign and profile context, digital journey and purchase-intent behav-
ior, and transactional retail activity. This combination provides a credible multi-source
basis for future Customer 360 analysis while remaining compatible with the available
infrastructure.
The project does not implement downstream machine-learning modules such as dynamic
segmentation, churn or lifetime-value prediction, persona generation, and recommenda-
tion engines. It delivers the distributed and secured data foundation required for those
capabilities, thereby keeping the report focused on the completed engineering contribution.
I.3.6 Implemented Outcomes and Academic Contribution
The completed outcome is a reproducible end-to-end platform in which heterogeneous
customer datasets are ingested, persisted, processed, transformed, queried, validated,
orchestrated, monitored, and secured. It provides replayable bronze artifacts in distributed
storage, governed analytical tables accessible through SQL, persisted quality evidence,
repeatable workflow execution, and observable collaboration across multiple nodes.
The academic contribution lies in integrating event transport, distributed storage and
computation, lakehouse table management, metadata governance, analytical access, trans-
formation control, quality assurance, orchestration, observability, deployment, and security
hardening into one consistent customer-data platform. Its originality is architectural and
engineering-oriented: the work demonstrates how these concerns can be combined and
operated coherently within a constrained multi-node environment.
I.4 Conclusion
This chapter established the organizational, business, and academic framing of AdOptimizer
CDP. It clarified the role of SMART AUTOMATION TECHNOLOGIES, distinguished
the project from the broader AdOptimizer AI vision, defined the engineering problem, and
stated the implemented objectives, scope, boundaries, and contribution. Chapter II now
develops the theoretical foundations that justify the distributed lakehouse architecture
and its principal design choices.
