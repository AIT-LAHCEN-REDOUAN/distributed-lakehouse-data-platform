II.2.3 Reliability, Scalability, and Maintainability
The literature on distributed systems frequently emphasizes three central qualities: reli-
ability, scalability, and maintainability [22]. Reliability refers to the system’s ability to
continue providing correct service despite component failures, abnormal workloads, or
operational disturbances. Scalability refers to the platform’s capacity to grow without dis-
proportionate degradation or redesign. Maintainability refers to the ability to understand,
operate, extend, and debug the system over time.
These qualities are fundamental for a platform such as AdOptimizer CDP. An unreliable
platform cannot support trusted analytical reuse. A non-scalable platform cannot absorb
new datasets or future use cases. A non-maintainable platform becomes fragile and difficult
to operate. These qualities are therefore direct evaluation criteria for the engineering
relevance of the project.
II.2.4 Data-Intensive Systems as a Distinct Engineering Problem
Data-intensive systems differ from ordinary software systems because data movement,
storage, replayability, and consistency become first-class engineering concerns. In such
systems, correctness is not defined only by the logic of business functions. It also depends
on whether data is durably persisted, whether transformations are reproducible, whether
schemas remain interpretable over time, and whether the system can explain the origin
and lifecycle of the information it exposes.
This distinction is important for the positioning of the present work. AdOptimizer CDP is
not simply an application with a database behind it. It is a distributed data engineering
platform whose value lies in organizing the lifecycle of heterogeneous customer-related
data in a reliable, observable, and governable way.
