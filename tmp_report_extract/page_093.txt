X.3 Future Perspectives
The next developments should preserve the current architecture’s separation of concerns
while increasing its availability, data coverage, governance maturity, and downstream
business value. The perspectives can be organized into three successive directions.
X.3.1 Platform Industrialization
The first priority is to strengthen the operating foundation before expanding business
functionality. Central coordinator and catalog responsibilities should be made more resilient,
and the local control plane should evolve toward a managed deployment environment.
Backup, restoration, failover, and node-loss scenarios should be tested explicitly rather
than inferred from successful nominal execution.
Deployment automation can also be extended through versioned environment configuration,
infrastructure provisioning, compatibility checks, and repeatable certificate and service-
identity initialization. Centralized secret management, automated certificate rotation,
finer-grained access control, and encryption enabled consistently across persistent runtime
storage would move the current security baseline closer to enterprise operation.
Performance evaluation should become systematic. Repeated benchmarks over identical
datasets and workflows can compare local and distributed execution, measure end-to-end
duration and service-level throughput, and identify CPU, memory, storage, and network
bottlenecks. Such measurements would guide capacity planning without replacing the
functional evidence already established by the project.
