• Interoperability:processing, table management, metadata, and query technologies
had to exchange data through compatible interfaces rather than through isolated
proprietary paths.
• Replayability and reproducibility:ingestion and transformation stages had to
support controlled re-execution, explicit dependencies, and repeatable deployment.
• Operational evidence:the platform had to expose observable service state, workflow
history, validation results, and distributed-execution evidence.
• Security integration:identity, browser access, remote submission, transport, and
runtime persistence had to be protected as part of the deployed architecture.
• Academic clarity:each technology had to have a precise architectural responsibility
that could be explained and defended without hiding the system behind a managed
platform.
These constraints led to five principal evaluation criteria: functional suitability, distributed
compatibility, cross-tool integration, operational reproducibility, and feasibility within the
available infrastructure. Community maturity and documentation quality were considered
supporting factors, but they were not treated as substitutes for technical fit.
III.3 Comparative Assessment
The alternatives in Table III.1 were not rejected as universally inferior technologies. They
were assessed against the narrower needs of the implemented AdOptimizer CDP platform.
The decisive question was therefore not which technology is best in every context, but
which combination best supports the required architecture with the least conceptual and
operational divergence.
